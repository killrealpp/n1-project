## Practical Findings

- Dmitriev treats the card/title/preview as the main traffic gate. CTR matters before the article can earn reading time.
- Strong Dzen headlines are concrete rather than generic: named people, assets, numbers, amounts, dates, mileage, time periods, or visible consequences.
- Useful headline models include: content-insight list plus number; short quote plus explanation; question/pronoun plus condition; fact/event plus deepening question; first/third-person experience; Soviet-era angle; exact numbers; negative consequence; experiment result.
- A title hook must be represented in the article body. A fake quote, exaggerated result, or absent payoff becomes clickbait.
- For new/thematic channels, he recommends narrow rubrics during warm-up, ideas from outside Dzen when possible, and original synthesis from several sources instead of one-to-one rewrite.
- For publishing time, he recommends the broad Moscow window from 06:00 to 22:00, avoiding 22:00-06:00 for peace of mind. There is no universal golden hour; content quality and the card/article bundle matter more. If publishing two materials, stronger one can go early and weaker one around 16:00-18:00.
- For AI content, he still keeps idea selection and headline judgement as high-value human/editorial tasks. AI drafts need cleanup: remove generic phrasing, awkward metaphors, unnatural punctuation, and unsupported details.
- One case post reports 10-12 minutes to produce one AI-assisted article, and 2-3 articles per day in 25-35 minutes, but this relies on rewrite-heavy workflows. For this project, use the productivity lesson, not the copying behavior.
